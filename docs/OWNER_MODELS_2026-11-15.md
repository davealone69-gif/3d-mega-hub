# The owner's 3D reference models ("3d real"), 2026-11-15

The owner shared a Dropbox folder named `3d real` with seven GLB files: three different models, some of them twice. This file records what those models are, what they show about getting to realistic avatars, and what the app does with them.

**The files themselves are not in this repository and not in any APK.** Only their measurements are recorded here. All three depict adult women, unclothed. They were inspected by structure and by untextured shape renders.

Status words are the project's. "Host" means the engine's importer run on a Linux host; nothing here has been run on the phone.

## 2. What they show about "real"

"Ink" is the realistic one, and it is realistic for reasons that have little to do with triangle count:

| | "Ink" | An avatar generated by this app (`2920265`) |
|---|---|---|
| Triangles in the body skin | **27,292** | 1,148,034 for the whole avatar |
| Triangles in hair and eyebrows | 443,084 (hair 273,332, eyebrows 169,752) | part of the same single mesh |
| Skin textures | four regions at 4096 x 4096 (face, torso, arms, legs) | one 2048 x 2048 atlas for body, hair and clothes together |
| Where the form comes from | a sculpted, professionally authored mesh | formulas |
| Eyes, teeth, lashes | separate parts with their own materials | painted on the body shell |
| Hair | cards and strands with transparency | a solid shape |

This is the measurement behind the direction in [PHOTOREAL_UPGRADE_PLAN.md](PHOTOREAL_UPGRADE_PLAN.md): an authored body of a few tens of thousands of triangles with large skin textures, separate eyes and card hair. The app's own avatar spends forty times the triangles on the body and gets a worse result.

The other two models are not a route to realism. The AI mesh has one small texture for the whole body and no skeleton. "Heather" is a statue in one pose.

"Ink" is also not usable as it is:

- **No morph targets.** The creator's sliders (body type, breast size and so on) cannot change it. In Daz Studio those shapes exist as morphs, and they were not exported.
- **No animation** in the file, and its skeleton is not the engine's, so the app's 15 clips do not fit it without retargeting.
- **Too heavy as exported.** Its textures need 1,152 MB once decoded with mipmaps. The eyebrows alone are 169,752 triangles.

## 3. Licence position

- **"Ink" is Daz content.** The skeleton is named `Genesis8Female` and the bones and material zones are that figure's. Its hair, brows and anatomy add-on carry the names of products sold for it. Whoever uploaded it to Sketchfab could not give a CC-BY licence to content they do not own, so the label does not make it free to ship. To ship this tier, the owner needs the figure and each product with a licence that allows use in an app (for Daz's own store that is the Interactive Licence, bought per product; other stores have their own terms), and an export from their own Daz Studio (see the plan, section 5.3).
- **"Heather"**: origin unknown. Treat the label with the same caution.
- **The AI mesh**: the uploader's own generation; CC-BY 4.0 with credit to milan037 is plausible.
- This repository and its releases are public. None of the three goes into either. Loading a file from the phone's own storage with **Import GLB** keeps it on the phone.

Not legal advice.

## 4. What the app does with them

Tested with the engine's importer on a host at `e4655c3`. The published build `phone-test-2920265` has the same importer.

| | "Nude girl T-pose" | "Heather" | "Ink" |
|---|---|---|---|
| Import | accepted | accepted | accepted, 7.2 s and 962 MB peak memory on the host |
| Shown by | the app's own renderer | the app's own renderer | the app's own renderer |
| Parts that get their texture | 1 of 1 | **2 of 14** | **4 of 45** |
| Transparency | not needed | ignored | ignored: hair drawn as solid sheets |
| Skeleton | none | none | not adopted: shown static |

The app's own importer keeps one colour texture for a whole model. That is enough for an avatar the app generated and for a single-texture scan. A model built from many textured parts comes out mostly grey.

Two further faults were found on the way:

- The app's second renderer (Filament) draws such files correctly, but since `210ae9a` the app sends an imported file there only when it carries animation clips. A static model with many textures stays on the renderer that cannot show it.
- Nothing limits texture memory. Sending "Ink" to the Filament preview by hand asks for 1,152 MB of textures, and the preview reads the whole 103.6 MB file into the Java heap twice. On the phone that is likely to end the app. (Inferred from the code and the file; not tried on the phone.)

## 5. What was changed (`7e4f0cb`)

| Change | Where | Checked how |
|---|---|---|
| A file not made by this app that has animation clips, **more than one colour texture or a transparent material** opens in the glTF viewer (Filament). Single-texture models and avatars made here stay on the app's own renderer | `GlbPreviewRouting.kt` | 13 JVM unit tests, 6 of them new |
| At import, the pixel size of every embedded image is read from its header. If the decoded textures (RGBA with mipmaps) exceed **448 MiB**, the largest are halved until they fit, and the app keeps a copy of the file with only those images replaced. The file the user picked is not touched | `GlbTextureBudget.kt`, `ImportedGlbPreparation.kt`, `MainActivity.kt` | 16 JVM unit tests with synthetic files and a stand-in for the image scaler |
| The image scaler itself: sampled decode, straight alpha, PNG kept for transparency and for normal maps, JPEG otherwise | `AndroidGlbImageScaler.kt` | **no test**; compiled and linted only |
| The glTF viewer refuses a file whose textures are still over the budget, decides before reading the file, reads it with one allocation, and reports running out of memory as a status line | `GlbPreviewRouting.kt`, `FilamentPreviewView.kt` | the refusal rule is unit-tested; the rest has no test |
| The glTF viewer can be turned with one finger, zoomed with two and reset with a double tap; it has ambient light beside its single directional light | `FilamentPreviewView.kt` | **no test** |

The texture reduction was also run on the real file, with a prototype of the same algorithm in Python on the host (the Kotlin code ran only against synthetic files):

| "Ink" | As downloaded | After the reduction |
|---|---|---|
| Textures | twelve at 4096, six at 2048 | eighteen at 2048 |
| Decoded with mipmaps | 1,152 MiB | 384 MiB |
| File | 103.6 MB | 60.8 MB |
| Geometry buffer views | 6 | the same 6, byte for byte |
| Engine importer on the host | 7.2 s, 962 MB peak | 2.0 s, 364 MB peak |

What to expect on the phone, none of it seen yet:

- **"Nude girl T-pose"**: as before, in the app's own renderer.
- **"Heather"**: opens in the glTF viewer with all 14 parts textured.
- **"Ink"**: the import takes longer (twelve large images are decoded and encoded once), the status line says "12 of 18 textures reduced", and it opens in the glTF viewer with all its materials and its hair cards transparent. It stands in its A-pose: the file has no animation.

What the glTF viewer still lacks: tone mapping, anti-aliasing, shadows and reflections (Filament's post-processing is switched off), and the skin, eye and hair shading of the app's own renderer.

CI results for `7e4f0cb` and the APK are in the README.
